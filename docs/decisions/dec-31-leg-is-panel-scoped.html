DEC-31 — one selector owns the leg, and every zone declares whether it follows one

A detail panel showing a captured exchange has two kinds of fact: those belonging to the flow, and those belonging to one of its two legs. Mixing the scopes is what produced two competing leg switches on one screen.

DEC-31 · 2026-09-05 · Trawler · E-REDESIGN.W5 · distilled from the flow-detail design run (Tune 8, Tune 9) · status: active

Context

The shipped flow detail panel answered "which leg am I looking at?" twice, differently. Its header tables stacked both legs at once — Request headers above Response headers — while the body pane beneath them carried its own request/response toggle (BodyViewer.tsx:148, :196–201 as it stood at ca18daa — W8 has since deleted it, which is this decision taking effect). Nothing connected the two: scrolling past both header tables and then switching the body's leg was the only way to read one leg's headers beside that leg's body.

Two models of one concept is not a layout defect — it is a vocabulary defect. A reader cannot learn the rule, because there isn't one. The screen was not showing "both legs at once"; only half of it was, and the half that wasn't never said so.

The E-REDESIGN.W5 design run met this twice. Its first pass refused a leg switch, weighing it against a Frame goal phrased as "compare the two legs" — and never checked whether the panel already contradicted that goal. It did. The refusal was reversed on the second reviewer round, once the shipped toggle was read in the code rather than assumed absent.

The decision

QuestionSettled
Who owns leg selection? The panel. One selector, in the pinned identity row, governing every leg-scoped zone at once.
What does the body pane own? Its own concerns only — decode (raw | decoded), the renderer dispatch, copy and save, its absences and its cut marker. It receives the leg as a prop and selects nothing.
What must every zone declare? Its scope: flow-scoped (identical on both legs) or leg-scoped (follows the selector). This is the load-bearing half — it is what makes the first two answers derivable rather than arbitrary.

The two scopes, on the panel this was decided for

┌─────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ POST 201 [request│response] 148 ms      ✕   │ ← the selector rides in the
├─────────────────────────────────────────────┤   row it scopes
│ https://api.example.io/v2/users?id=7        │ flow-scoped — one uri, one
│ HTTP/1.1 → HTTP/2       [ copy as curl ]    │ curl command, both legs'
├─────────────────────────────────────────────┤ versions. Stated ONCE, and
│ HEADERS                                     │ deliberately unlabelled
│   host          api.example.io              │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────┤ leg-scoped — replaced when
│ BODY                                        │ the selector moves. These
│   ░░░ the selected leg's body ░░░           │ two are the ONLY ones
└─────────────────────────────────────────────┘

A flow-scoped zone carries no leg label. That is not a style preference: a "Request" heading over the uri repeated the identity row when request was selected and contradicted the data when response was. The absence of the label is what keeps the scope legible.

What was rejected, and why

AlternativeWhy it lost
Keep both legs stacked
the shipped behaviour		 It was never the actual behaviour — the body already toggled. Keeping it would have meant keeping the contradiction, and it forces the reader to scroll one unbounded header collection to reach the other.
A tablist
tabs owning panels		 SegmentedControl's @avoid excludes "tabs that own panels (tablist)". The zones are the screen's, not a tab's subtree; this is a radiogroup that switches content, which is what the component is for.
A leg switch per zone
headers and body each		 The defect, formalised. Two controls for one concept, able to disagree.
Leave it to the body run (W7)
where the epic plan had put it		 W7 owns BodyViewer's internals; leg is not one. A body-run decision cannot fix a panel-level contradiction, and W7 designing a control the panel had already subsumed is precisely the collision a seam exists to prevent.
