DEC-32 — a detail panel never scrolls as a whole: the body is its main zone, and every other zone is bounded

A panel is a fixed window onto unbounded capture data. When one collection is allowed to grow, the zone a reader came for ends up below it — and, in WebKit, so does the window.

DEC-32 · 2026-09-05 · Trawler · E-REDESIGN.W6 · distilled from the owner's round on the built flow detail · status: active · amends the flow-detail design run's Layout (its scroll rule and its "no cap" on the header collection) and its "wrap always; never clip" constraint

Context

W5's design gave the flow detail one scroll region for everything under the identity row — flow line, headers, body — and refused to cap the header collection: "read the wire truth: every header rendered, nothing folded away". W6 built it. Against real traffic the owner found what the design's fixtures could not show: a response with three session cookies, a bearer token and a content-security-policy renders as a header table taller than four screens, in a 380px pane where a 900-character cookie wraps to thirty lines. The body — the zone the reader clicked the row to see — sat below all of it. And in WebKit the overflow of that table leaked past its scroll container into the document, so the window gained four screens of empty scroll below the app.

The design was right about the data and wrong about the space. Every header must still be readable in full, in wire order, with duplicates preserved, and copyable whole. What a fixed-height panel cannot do is give every collection its natural height at once. Something has to be bounded, and the question is what.

The decision

QuestionSettled
Does the panel scroll? Never as a whole. Each zone is either fixed-height chrome or a scroll container of its own. A reader always sees every zone's label and the start of its content.
Which zone is primary? The body. It takes whatever height the other zones leave — flex-1 — and scrolls inside that. The cut marker at its foot stays visible for the same reason W8 gave: the pane is a bounded box, whatever its height.
How big may a secondary zone get? The headers take their natural height up to a share of the panel (max-h-zone-cap, 35%), then scroll inside; the heading stays and names how many rows there are. Identity row and flow line are chrome at natural height.
What about one value that is itself a screen tall? Clamped to three lines, with more / less under its name. The clamp hides lines, never characters: the value is still the verbatim payload, still copies whole on click, and one act shows all of it. That is a named, reversible view in the sense of display-equals-payload, not a truncation.
Where does "which zone is main" live? In the screen. Components stay layout-neutral; a host sizes a Section through its className and a pane fills what it is given.

What was rejected, and why

AlternativeWhy it lost
Keep one scroll region the design as written Correct for the data, wrong for the reader: the primary zone's position depends on how many cookies a server set. The design's own goal ranking put "read the wire truth" above the body only because the body was another screen's; on the built panel they compete for one height.
Tabs Headers | Body, one at a time The devtools idiom, and it would solve the height fight — but the panel already carries one selector (the leg, DEC-31), and a second axis of switching on a 380px pane hides the zone a reader most often wants beside the headers. Bounded zones show both.
Truncate long values single line, ellipsis The design's explicit ✗: "ellipsis on a JWT". Characters would be hidden with no way back but copying. The clamp hides lines and offers the rest in one act.
A fixed pixel cap on the headers Deterministic but blind to the window: 256px is a third of a laptop panel and a sixth of a desktop one. A share keeps the body the larger zone at every height.
Fix the window scroll alone position the scroll container, keep the layout Necessary — the WebKit leak is real and the fix (relative on the scroll container) ships — but it would leave the body four screens down.

Every scroll container holding a KeyValueRow grid Is position: relative. WebKit lets a static scroll container's overflow leak into the document's scrollable area when its content is a display:contents grid; bisected live in the Tauri window (document scroll height 4477 → 768 with the one property).
Any later detail screen Name its primary zone before laying it out; give every other zone a bound. A second sighting of this rule is what would lock it into the rulebook as a slug; with DEC-31 this panel now carries two decisions that a future run should read before its Layout stage.

Two tokens, one scale. value-clamp (60px: three wire lines plus the focus ring's room inside the clipping cell) joins spacing; maxHeight is now a declared scale — spacing plus full plus zone-cap (35%) — so the closed-vocabulary lint sees it. Nothing else moved: no colour, no type, no new layout step.
