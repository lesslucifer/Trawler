DEC-47 — severity decides the verdict, and a nit is dispositioned evidence

E-SIMPWF.W5 stops the review collecting a severity on every finding and then discarding it. A verified blocker or important fails the verdict; a verified nit does not, and instead travels in the note with what was decided about it — so one small finding stops buying a second fleet round.

Decided 2026-09-13 · E-SIMPWF.W5 · active

The decision

A review's verdict is decided by severity. PASS = Gate 0 green and zero verified blockers or importants left unwaived. A verified nit never fails a verdict.

A nit is recorded and decided, not dropped. Every verified nit goes in the cr/v4 note's nits: list as a finding plus one disposition — fixed: "<what changed>", meaning the fix is already inside the range the note binds, or waived: "<reason>". An empty sentence is no disposition, exactly as an empty test-layer waiver is no waiver, and the merge gate refuses it under 3·review-nits — along with an entry whose finding names nothing, and one carrying both dispositions, which records a contradiction rather than a decision.

The boundary is written where both agents read it. rubric.md carries the three severity rows; the finder proposes a severity and the verifier's is the one the verdict reads, because only the verifier read the surrounding code. Uncertainty resolves upward, to important.

Nothing about the binding changes. A fix landing after the verdict still moves CODE_SHA and still reds 5·diff-binding. This record makes a small finding cheap to answer; it does not make a post-verdict commit cheap.

Why it needed deciding

Severity was already collected twice — once by the finder, once by the verifier — and then read by nobody: the verdict failed on any verified finding left unwaived. A nit therefore had exactly two exits, and both were wrong. Failing the tip meant a commit, and a commit moves the code hash, which orphans the PASS and buys five finders and their verifiers again for a renamed variable. Waiving it through --waive recorded a nit in the same shape as a waived blocker, so the note could no longer tell a reader which had happened.

The epic measured where its time actually goes: the review fleet is a stable ~25% of a gate-heavy epic's active agent time (DEC-11), and E-RFINISH needed a second round on one Working Unit and three rounds to close. A round bought by a finding nobody thinks is worth a commit is the cheapest of those rounds to stop buying.

The risk, and what holds it

The riskWhat holds it
Severity becomes the escape hatch: a real finding relabelled nit to get a tip through The boundary is stated, not assumed — nit is the narrow exit, reached only by surviving nothing observable changes and nothing drifts; uncertainty resolves upward to important
The finder's optimism decides the verdict The verifier's severity is what the verdict reads. The finder's is a proposal, re-judged by the agent that read the code
The nits: list becomes an unread dumping ground Every entry must be decided. An undecided one is a red gate that names the finding — the pass is cheap, the decision is not optional
Recall falls Measured before adoption against known-real findings, per the epic's stopping rule: a finding that fails today landing as a nit drops the rule rather than arguing for it

Why this shape

ChoiceWhy
A nits: section, not a waiver A waiver says a real failure was accepted; a nit was never a failure. Keeping them apart is what lets a reader of the note tell the two apart, and what keeps the waiver bill meaningful
cr/v4 absorbs it — no schema bump An absent section already means what every existing note means: no nits recorded. A bump would re-review in-flight tips to say something no reader would learn
Empty sentence = no disposition One rule, already in the tree for test-layer waivers, walk waivers and design drops: recording that something happened is not recording why
fixed means inside the bound range Any other meaning would quietly license a commit after the verdict, which the binding still refuses. The cost of a post-verdict fix is a separate question

Rejected

AlternativeWhy not
Let a nit pass with no record at all The saving is the same and the evidence is gone: nobody can later ask what the round found, and a real finding mislabelled a nit leaves no trace to audit
Keep waiving nits through --waive It is what happens today. It costs a declaration per nit at the trigger, and it records a nit as though a failure had been accepted
Stop finders reporting nits at all The cheapest findings are often the ones a reader wants; and a finder told not to report small things will also under-report the ones that turn out not to be small
Let the trigger session fix nits after the verdict without re-review That breaks the binding, which is the one thing making a PASS mean anything. Making a post-verdict fix cheap is a scoped re-verification question, not a severity one
