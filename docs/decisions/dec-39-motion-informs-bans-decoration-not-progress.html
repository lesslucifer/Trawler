DEC-39 — motion-informs bans decoration, not progress: a spinner is allowed

Two design runs read the rule as a ban on spinners and receipted it that way. Both went further than the rule they were applying. This records the reading that stands, so a third run does not re-derive it.

Decided 2026-09-08 · E-EXPORT.W6 · supersedes run 10 and run 11 on this slug alone

The decision

An in-flight indicator may animate. motion-informs forbids animation that carries no information; a turning ring reports that an act the user started is still running, which is a state change and the one fact they are waiting on. What the rule still forbids is decoration — a hover glow, a panel sliding for style — and what it still requires is that the screen not move: the indicator occupies a reserved box and displaces nothing around it.

Why it needed deciding

WhereWhat it said
Run 10 (2026-09-05-export-run-10)"nothing animates; in-flight is a disabled control plus a static word, never a spinner or a bar", and in its stage log, "the reason it is a word and not a spinner".
Run 11 (2026-09-06-export-run-11), which supersedes run 10"nothing animates, and nothing moves" — carrying its predecessor's verdict forward without re-testing it, which is the failure mode DEC-38 exists to catch.
The rulebook itself, unchanged throughout"Motion and transience only communicate state change; nothing animates for decoration; transient feedback expires (~2 s)." Its reviewer question: "For each animation: what does it tell the user? 'Nothing' fails."

The rule and the receipts disagreed, and the receipts were the stricter of the two. Because a receipt is where a reviewer looks for a slug's settled reading, two runs' worth of over-application had hardened into an apparent prohibition that the rule's own text never carried.

The reading, against the rule's three clauses

ClauseApplied to a progress ring
"only communicate state change"Passes. A running transfer is a state, its running-ness is the change, and the ring is the only thing on screen reporting it continuously. Answer the rule's own question — what does this tell the user? — and the answer is "your export has not finished", which is not nothing.
"nothing animates for decoration"Not engaged. This is the clause both runs leaned on, and it is conditional: it bans animation for decoration, not animation. The DON'T the rule ships — hover glow, panels sliding for style — is the decoration it means.
"transient feedback expires (~2 s)"Not engaged. It governs an acknowledgement that must not linger, which is why the rule's own DO is a confirm chip that appears and expires. A progress indicator's duration belongs to the transfer, not the designer; a ring that expired after two seconds while a 73MB export continued would report the opposite of the truth.

What the runs got right, and is kept

Their real concern was a screen that twitches while the user is reading it, and that concern is upheld as a constraint on any in-flight indicator:

It may turn

Animation confined to the indicator itself.

It may not move anything

Run 11's "nothing moves" stands in full. The indicator sits in a box whose width is reserved whether or not it is drawn, so arriving and leaving displaces no neighbour — the same reason the capture header's count slot is fixed-width.

It is never the only channel

A word names what is awaited beside it. The ring is aria-hidden; the sentence is what a screen reader gets.

Scope

This settles the slug's reading, not a screen's design. A run may still decide a particular surface is better served by a word alone — what it may no longer do is cite motion-informs as the reason it had no choice. The rulebook text is unchanged because nothing in it was wrong; what was wrong was two applications of it, and both receipts now record the reversal in place rather than losing it.
