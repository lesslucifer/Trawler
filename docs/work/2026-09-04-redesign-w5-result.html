Three reviewer rounds that corrected the design

The reviewer saidWhat it changed, and why the author was wrong
"the curl copy should be a button only, users don't really need to see the curl content" Applied. It improved the component fit rather than bending it: CopyButton's @use is a payload that is "derived/invisible", which became literally true, and its @avoid on the icon-only form then forbade a glyph — so the label had to carry its own noun. A later round removed the titled Section around it too: a heading over one self-labelling button is the redundancy labels-add-context names.
"I still think we need a tabs or kind of switch between request / response … Same for the body" Reversed the author's refusal. The decisive fact was in the code, not the argument: BodyViewer already shipped a request/response toggle (BodyViewer.tsx:148, :196–201), so the panel was never showing "both legs at once" — its headers were, while the body beneath them toggled. The first refusal weighed the switch against a goal phrased as "compare the two legs" and never checked whether the screen already contradicted it.
"please rethink thoroughly about the overall layout & UI UX best practices … Do not just focus on one problem" Fair on both counts. The rounds had been patching one fault at a time. Looking at the panel whole found three faults with one cause — two stacked pinned bars, a "Request" zone that never followed the selector (introduced by the author in the previous round), and two headings for one leg — all because every zone was being treated as leg-scoped when only two are. That became the Frame driver the layout now derives from.

Deviations

#DeviationWhy
D1 The epic plan's W5 row predicted HeaderTable would resolve to reuse; it resolves to a T3 local. No registry entry owns a pair grid. D2 The WU edited the epic plan, a shared file W7 and W8 read. Required by round 1's blocker. W2's precedent (d7f186c) is explicit: a WU that changes what a later WU owns corrects the rows in its own WU.
D3 The WU opened a T2 decision record (DEC-31), which a design WU does not normally do. The distillation checklist's "Non-obvious choice made or reversed? → New decisions/ record" row is unconditional. Two rounds' worth of reasoning to the contrary was wrong: the first cited DEC-16 (which governs component design-file retirement), the second borrowed a "second sighting" threshold from the design-rules row.
D4 The seam moved: leg selection is the panel's, so W7 loses a subject the epic plan gave it. A body run cannot fix a panel-level contradiction, and W7 designing a control the panel had already subsumed is the collision a seam exists to prevent.
D5 Two receipt verdicts are blocked, not satisfied. Esc is designed per the rulebook's own sighting on this panel, but FlowDetail.tsx binds no key handler. A designed path is not a shipped one, and satisfied there would be the "complete, fluent and false" receipt the Tier-3 review exists to catch. W6 owns making both true.
