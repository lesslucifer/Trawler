The review rounds

Three finders over the diff's code paths each round, Gate 0 green beside them. Ten rounds in three series. Rounds 1 to 6 judged the first half and are below as they were written. Rounds 7 and 8 judged the whole unit again after the window round, and rounds 9 and 10 again after the rhythm round, because the note binds the range whole and a verdict bound to an earlier tip says nothing about bytes that landed after it.

Round 9 is the answer to a fair question about re-reviewing. The rhythm round changed a container's spacing and one test, and re-running the fleet over the whole range for it looks like ceremony. It found a real important in the first half's code — a reachable action state with no note — that eight previous rounds over the same bytes had not raised. The re-review is not ceremony because the note binds the range and not the commit; what round 9 demonstrates is the weaker and more useful fact that a fleet's coverage of a fixed diff is not monotone, and the rounds a moved tip buys are not wasted on the bytes that did not move.

The two series found different things, and the difference is the point. The first half's six rounds found five real defects, every one of them a sentence the screen was not entitled to say. The window round's two rounds found none: it moved no claim, only how much of the screen says them at once, so there was no new claim to be wrong about. What the second series did produce is a test — see round 7 — which is what a round is worth when it finds nothing.
