The owner's round — real traffic, four complaints, one cause

Raised after the first tip was merged, against the running app with a browser's traffic through it. The merge was undone, the mr/v1 and dod/v1 notes withdrawn, and the round landed on the same WU branch.

What was raisedWhat it turned out to be, and what changed
"Users need to scroll a long time to get to the body — which is the most important part." The header zone was unbounded, as the design asked. A design decision that its fixtures could not test. Three session cookies, a bearer token and a CSP make a header table four screens tall in a 380px pane. Changed: the panel never scrolls as a whole. Identity row and flow line are chrome; the headers Section takes its natural height up to 35% of the panel and scrolls inside (heading pinned, now naming the row count); the body Section is flex-1 and its pane scrolls inside. Under the wall of headers the e2e asserts the body zone is the taller of the two and ends on screen.
"Some values are really long and consume a full screen — cookies, tokens." Thirty wrapped lines per value, and the design's "wrap always; never clip" made every one of them load-bearing. Changed: KeyValueRow gains collapsible: a value that overflows three lines is clamped, with more / less under its name. Measured, not guessed — no toggle when nothing is hidden. The clamp hides lines, never characters; click still copies the whole value and the confirm tones the visible lines, so display-equals-payload holds as a named, reversible view. Tabs and ellipsis were considered and rejected — the reasoning is DEC-32's.
"The body should be the main part and have the largest space." W8's pane was capped at a fixed 256px so the cut marker stayed visible; on a desktop window most of the panel was empty. Changed: the pane fills the height its host gives (flex-1 min-h-0) — a bounded box at any height keeps the marker at its foot, so W8's reason survives the change. Section gains className so a host can size a zone; the slot stays layout-neutral.
"The main screen is also scrollable and users can scroll down into a large empty space." Reproduced in the real window and bisected live: with the long-header fixture the document's scroll height was 4477px against a 768px window; hiding the value cells, or giving the scroll container position: relative, brought it to 768. A WebKit quirk — a static scroll container's overflow leaks into the document's scrollable area when its content is a display:contents grid, which is exactly what KeyValueRow is. Changed: every scroll container holding that grid is relative (the headers box and the body pane, for form bodies), and the e2e asserts the document scroll height equals the window height under the wall of headers.

This amends the design it cites. Three lines of W5's Layout — one scroll region owns every collection; the header collection has no cap; wrap always, never clip — are overturned by the owner's decision, for a reason the design could not see from its fixtures. The archived run is not edited; the standing rule is DEC-32, with the rejected alternatives (one scroll region, tabs, ellipsis, a pixel cap, positioning alone). A build that silently departs from its design's Layout is what the citation exists to make visible, so it is recorded here rather than smoothed over — the same way W8 recorded its overturned Frame constraint.

Deviations

What changedWhy
A jsdom polyfill in ui/src/setup-tests.ts. Range.prototype.getClientRects and getBoundingClientRect now exist under the test environment, returning nothing. CodeMirror measures text on the frame after it mounts, and jsdom's Range has neither method, so that frame always throws. On the base it threw into the virtual console and the suite exited 0. On this branch the App test that reaches the response body mounts the editor after a click — one act later than before — and the pending frame outlived the test file, which turned the same error into an unhandled one and pnpm test into exit 1. Verified both ways: the base worktree exits 0 with the identical stderr line; this branch exited 1 before the polyfill and 0 after. The polyfill is the honest fix — a jsdom gap closed once for every test — rather than re-timing one test around it.
The curl e2e selects the button by its zone. $('[data-testid="flow-line"] button'), not button=copy as curl. Two e2e runs each failed exactly one case, mine both times, in the same test. First, wdio's tag=text selector matches direct text nodes, and CopyButton nests its label in spans; the SegmentedControl segments have direct text, which is why the same idiom works for them. Second, WebKit's getText reports the visibility:hidden widest-label span too — the one that keeps the button from resizing when it reads copied — so the text came back as copy as curlcopy as curl. The spec now matches the label rather than equating it, before and after the click. Product code untouched by either fix.
Two test hooks the design does not mention. data-testid="flow-line" on zone B, and data-testid="detail-loading" kept on a plain wrapper around the pending Notice. The integration and e2e tests must read the flow line's text before and after a leg switch to prove it did not move, and the existing loading testid has three consumers. Both sit on elements the screen owns; no registry member gained a prop.
The T3 local is named LegHeaders. The design calls it "header table". The plan's expected-result row 9 says the file defines no HeaderTable, meaning the shipped inert one; a same-named replacement would have made that row unfalsifiable by grep. The name says what the old one did not: it renders the selected leg's headers.
Three registry members changed inside a build WU. KeyValueRow (collapsible), Section (className), BodyViewer (the pane fills its host). Two tokens added: value-clamp and a declared maxHeight scale with zone-cap. The plan said no member changes — a member that needs one is a finding. The owner's round made the finding: the panel's zoning cannot be expressed with layout-neutral members that own no height, and the clamp belongs to the component that owns the wire pair (FormRenderer's pairs have the same problem and get it for free, opt-in). Each contract line moved is stated in the member's docstring and in DEC-32's what this forces table; all three files are declared on the review note.
Existing tests rewritten, not extended. Two W8 integration tests and one W8 e2e asserted two body panes on the page; three App tests and one e2e asserted the curl testid. Each deleted assertion maps to a design line: the two-pane layout to DEC-31 (the W8 result named the panel's zoning as W6's), the rendered curl to Tune 7. Their replacements assert what the design put there instead — one selector moving both zones, and the payload itself.

What the design said not to do, and where the code shows it did not

The design's ruleWhat the code does
"A flow-scoped zone carries no leg label" — no Request heading over the URI. Zone B is a div, not a Section; the only headings on the panel are Headers and Body. A unit test asserts neither Request headers nor Response headers exists.
"One selector, never two." The panel holds the leg once and passes it to both leg-scoped zones. The unit test counts every radiogroup on the panel and finds the leg's and the pane's decode switch — nothing else.
"Nothing unbounded may enter row A." Row A mounts verb, status, the two-segment control, duration and a 28px button. The URI sits in B, wrapping, and scrolls away.
"Nothing moves on its own" — no live-ticking duration. DurationText receives flow.duration_ms and holds a transparent placeholder when it is null; the screen has no timer of any kind. Its only two transients are the components' own ~2 s confirms.
"The command is never rendered" (Tune 7). toCurl(flow) is called inside getPayload, at click time only. Unit and e2e both assert the panel's text contains no curl.
"Default leg is request, always" (DEC-31) — not the per-flow rule. useState<Leg>("request"); flow.response_body is read nowhere in the file.
No zebra, no eviction notice, no action slot on either Notice. None of the three exists in the file.
