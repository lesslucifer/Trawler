E3 · work-planning — plan outputs & result reports, checklist-verified

T3 plan: define what the planning phase of an epic/WU must output and what the result report after the work must contain — as minimum-content checklists with a defined way to verify each, never as fixed templates.

Draft v2 · 2026-07-05 · Trawler · T3 work doc · epic/work-planning

Epic requirement

Planning and result-reporting get defined outputs. For each output the T1 standard gives a checklist of what must at least be in it and a verification procedure that checks presence and quality of every item — while the form stays free: a plan or report may be prose, tables, diagrams or widgets, shaped creatively to fit its task. No fixed templates, no git-notes or hook machinery — this epic is about content standards and their verification, not about gates.

Expected result — observable

  • docs/work-planning.html (T1) exists and owns: the planning outputs of an epic and of a WU, the result-report outputs of an epic and of a WU, one minimum-content checklist per output, and the verification procedure for each checklist.
  • An agent starting any epic can produce a conforming plan — and close any epic/WU with a conforming result report — from the standard alone, without copying a skeleton.
  • Each checklist is verifiable: running the defined procedure on a document yields a pass, or concrete findings naming the missing/weak items.
  • workflow.html's plan-artifact cells link to the standard instead of half-defining it.

Non-goals

  • No fixed templates. The standard constrains substance (items that must exist), never form. A skeleton to copy-paste is explicitly what we don't want.
  • No git machinery. No new note schemas, no verify-dod checks, no hooks. Existing DoD gates stay untouched; wiring checklist verification into them is a possible later epic, not this one.
  • No tracker files or dashboards.
  • No work estimates anywhere in plans or reports (AI-first rule).

The gap

workflow.html mandates "one T3 work doc listing its WUs, each with requirement sentence + detailed test plan" — and stops there. E1's brainstorm and E2's plan already diverge in what they contain; nothing says what a complete plan holds, and nothing at all defines what to produce after the work: today an epic ends with evidence notes and distilled T2 docs, but no result report states what was delivered against what was planned, what deviated and why. Quality of plans and reports is judged by taste, per-session, with no checklist to judge against.

Proposed shape — four outputs, four checklists, one verification loop

flowchart LR
  EP["Epic plan"] -->|verify vs checklist| WORK["WUs execute"]
  WP["WU plan entries"] -->|verify vs checklist| WORK
  WORK --> WR["WU result report"]
  WR -->|verify vs checklist| NEXT["next WU"]
  NEXT --> WORK
  WORK --> ER["Epic result report"]
  ER -->|verify vs checklist| CLOSE["distill · archive · done"]
Every artifact is verified against its checklist before the work moves on: plans before code, result reports before close.

The four outputs. Planning phase: the epic plan (one T3 doc per epic) containing a plan entry per WU. After the work: a result report per WU (recorded when the WU completes) and an epic result report (at epic close). Result reports live in the epic's T3 doc or beside it — form and location flexible, the checklist is what's mandatory. All T3 lifecycle rules (distill, then archive) apply unchanged.

Draft checklists — v0, to be refined and owned by the T1. Each item must be present and substantive: specific to this task, observable where it claims to be, never boilerplate.

The verification way. A checklist is verified by a fresh-context agent that did not author the document, run at the loop's four points (plans before work starts, reports before the work is called done). The verifier walks the checklist as a rubric — for each item: is it present, is it substantive (task-specific, observable where required, not boilerplate), is it consistent with the other items — and returns pass or findings (item, what's missing/weak, concrete fix). The author fixes and re-verifies until pass. Quality is enforced by the second pair of eyes, not by parsing.

Design notes

Checklists over templates. A fixed template optimizes for uniformity and rots into cargo-cult sections; a checklist optimizes for completeness while letting each task pick the form that carries it best (E2's plan was right to be 57 terse lines; a protocol-heavy epic may need diagrams and a widget). The standard therefore never prescribes headings or order — only items, their meaning, and the quality bar.

Why agent-verification, not parsing. "Expected result is observable" or "deviation explains why" cannot be checked mechanically without forcing rigid structure — which is the rejected template in disguise. The house already trusts fresh-context judgment bound to a specific artifact (the code-review skill); checklist verification is the same pattern pointed at planning docs.

Relation to the DoD gates. None, deliberately. The workflow's mechanical gates keep verifying tests/review/docs evidence exactly as today. This standard is procedural: verification happens at the four loop points because the standard says so. If it earns its keep, hooking it into gates is a separate, later decision.

Open questions

  • Is W2 in scope? Proposed yes — a checklist nobody can run uniformly will drift. The lean alternative: the T1 defines the procedure prose-only and sessions spawn ad-hoc verifier agents; drop W2 if that's preferred.
  • Where do result reports live? Proposed: appended to the epic's plan doc (one place per epic, plan and results side by side); a separate result doc stays allowed when size warrants. Alternative: always separate docs.
  • Verification outcome recording. Proposed: one line in the verified doc (who/when/verdict) — enough to see it happened, no new evidence machinery. Alternative: none at all.
