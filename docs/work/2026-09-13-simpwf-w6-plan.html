E-SIMPWF.W6 — the fleet shrinks to what its numbers justify

A review round stops spawning one agent per candidate. Five dimension finders fold into two, the per-candidate verifiers fold into one that judges every candidate of the round, both prompts shrink to what the merged shape needs — and the round writes down what it cost, so the next cut is chosen from counts rather than from shape.

v1 · 2026-09-13 · Trawler · T3 WU plan · E-SIMPWF.W6 · wu/simpwf/fleet-weight off epic/simpwf · epic plan

Requirement

A review round spawns a fixed, small fleet: two finders — behaviour and shape, between them covering the same five dimensions and five principles rubric.md states today — and exactly one verifier, which receives every candidate of the round and returns a verdict, a severity and evidence for each. The verifier still never sees a finder's reasoning and still resolves uncertainty to false-positive; the severity rows, the exclusions and the verdict rule (DEC-47) are unchanged in what they say. Each round records what it cost on its own note under a fleet: block — finders spawned, verifier agents spawned, and candidates raised and verified per finder — carried by a cr/v5 contract, and cargo xtask tally --fleet reads those blocks back. The cut is adopted only against the A/B: every known-real finding in the corpus of § the A/B is raised and verified by the new fleet. A finding that escapes drops the cut — the shape reverts and the WU is recorded as measured-and-rejected, with the finding that killed it named.

The cut

Directed by the owner on 2026-09-13, not chosen by a measurement: reduce the number of finders, have only one verifier verify the findings of all finders, and simplify both prompts. The measurement is the guard that decides whether it is adopted, not the chooser of its shape.

The same rubric, the same severity rows, the same exclusions — carried by three agents instead of sixteen.
PieceTodayAfterWhy it is safe to merge
The finders Five, one per rubric.md dimension row Two. behaviour = correctness + efficiency, carrying principles P1–P4; shape = reuse + simplification + altitude, carrying P5 The split is the rubric's own seam: P1–P4 are all claims about what the code does when it runs, P5 is a claim about how it is put together. No dimension's question, catch or exclusion changes text — they are redistributed, not rewritten.
The verifiers One fresh agent per candidate, ~10.7 per round One, receiving every candidate of the round in a single batch and returning one {verdict, severity, evidence} per candidate Isolation from the finder's reasoning is what the adversarial property rests on, and that is preserved: the verifier receives claims and scope, never a finder's argument. Overlapping claims now dedup for free instead of buying two agents.
The prompts Five dimension rows read by five agents; a finder prompt naming its dimension, the rubric read, the scope and the return format Two merged rows; the finder prompt keeps the same four parts against the merged dimension, and the verifier prompt states the batch contract and the per-claim return Nothing is dropped from the rubric's content — the table is regrouped and the prose that only existed to coordinate five agents goes.

Arithmetic of the cut, against the measured averages above: 15.7 → 3 agents per round, an 81% reduction in agents spawned, and a constant fleet — a round's cost stops scaling with the number of candidates raised.

What the cut trades, stated before it is measured

TradeWhat could go wrongWhat answers it
Overlap as recall rubric.md says outright that "recall comes from five overlapping finders". Two sweeps overlap less than five. The A/B. This is the single largest risk in the WU and the thing the corpus exists to test.
Cross-contamination in one verifier Judging fifteen claims in one context invites anchoring — confirming a weak claim after a strong neighbour, or dismissing a real one after refuting several in a row. The verifier returns per-claim evidence, so a shallow judgment is visible in the note and in the A/B's failures. The batch is capped (below) so no single context carries an unbounded pile.
Depth per claim Today one agent reads the surrounding code for one claim. One agent reading it for fifteen may read each less deeply — the exact failure that would let a real finding be refuted. The batch cap: over 12 candidates the verifier runs in successive batches, still one agent-shape rather than one per candidate. 12 is above the measured mean of 10.7 and below the tail, so the common round stays a single batch.
Wall-clock, not agent count Ten parallel verifiers become one sequential agent. Agents spawned falls 81%; elapsed time may not fall with it, and could rise on a heavy round. Named here rather than hidden. The WU's claim is a cut in fleet cost; the result report states the wall-clock it actually observed alongside it, and does not claim a saving it did not measure.

The A/B — the measurement that decides it

The bar is the epic's: every known-real finding in the corpus is raised and verified by the new fleet. Not "most", and not "the new fleet finds interesting things" — a cut that loses one real finding is a cut that lowered the bar, and the stopping rule drops it.

Building the corpus

A known-real finding needs its claim in text and its diff on disk. Two sources exist in this repository; the third does not, and saying so is part of the plan.

SourceSizeHow a case is built
FAIL rounds followed by their fix 35 FAIL notes The note names the tip that failed; the corrective commit that followed is the finding, in code. The case is: the pre-fix diff, and the claim the fix answers.
Notes carrying named findings 26 notes with - finding: entries (waivers and, on cr/v4, nits) The claim is already written out, bound to a diff_sha256 that pins the exact bytes it was about.
Archived epic results None available Archived out of git at the epic gate (DEC-3); docs/.archive/ does not exist in this tree. The E-RFINISH numbers the epic plan cites cannot be re-derived here.

The run takes 8 cases, drawn across both sources and across dimensions so that each of the five original rows is represented at least once — a corpus that only exercised correctness would say nothing about folding altitude into shape. Each case is replayed at its own base, so the fleet sees the tree as the original round saw it.

The measurement is a gate on adoption, not a report written after it.
Recorded per caseWhy
Known-real findings raised / verified by the new fleetThe bar itself
Which finder raised each — behaviour or shapeTells whether the grouping is the right seam, and is the number a later regrouping would be chosen from
Candidates raised and refutedA collapse in refutation rate would mean the single verifier got permissive, not that the finders got precise
Agents spawned, and elapsed timeThe claimed saving, and the wall-clock trade named above

If the bar is missed. The escape is diagnosed once, because an escape has two possible causes and they have different answers: a finder that never raised the claim (the grouping is wrong — try three finders, splitting shape) or a verifier that refuted a real claim (the batch is too large — lower the cap). One re-measurement is taken against that diagnosis. If it still escapes, the cut is dropped whole: the skill and rubric revert, the cr/v5 instrument stays — it costs nothing and the epic promised the counts — and the epic result records W6 as measured-and-rejected with the finding that killed it.
