E-SIMPWF.W9 — the round is cut to what pays

W6 and W8 built the instrument and took the one cut that cost nothing. This WU spends the instrument: it reads 198 recorded rounds, retires the two finder dimensions that never produced a severe code finding, makes a third conditional, batches the verifier stage by the code a verifier must read, and stops the epic gate re-reviewing code its seams already cleared. The bar moves too — from every known-real finding is raised, which no scope cut can pass, to no verified blocker or important is lost.

v1 · 2026-09-15 · Trawler · T3 WU plan · E-SIMPWF.W9 · wu/simpwf/round-cut off epic/simpwf · DEC-48 · DEC-49 — the records this plan amends

Requirement

The finder stage is correctness and efficiency, with altitude spawned only where the diff touches more than one module; simplification and reuse are retired from the rubric, each beside the corpus number that retired it, so a later reading of "more coverage" cannot re-buy them. The verifier stage is batched by relevance — the orchestrator groups candidates that share the code a verifier must read — except that a candidate proposed blocker never joins a batch, so the one severity that decides a verdict keeps a fresh adversary of its own. The epic gate stops asking for a review of its own: 3·review, 3·review-nits, 4·review-base and 5·diff-binding leave the epic check list, while 7·walk keeps re-reading every seam's review, so an epic is still refused when a seam has lost its binding. Every round under the new shape writes stage_seconds and by_finder, so the next cut is chosen from the first real bills the tree has ever held. The bar is that no verified blocker or important is lost — not W6's "every known-real finding is raised", which is unpassable by construction for any cut that removes scope.

The verifier stage — batched by relevance

DEC-48 made "never batch the verifiers" a rule on W6's arm B: tokens halved, the stage ran 4.6× longer. That measurement stands, and it does not apply here.

W6's reasonDoes relevance-grouping answer it?
"A batched judge reads every claim's surrounding code in one context instead of several in parallel" Yes — this is the whole point. W6 batched the whole pool, undifferentiated, so one agent serially read every region the pool touched. A batch grouped by shared code reads that region once for all its claims. The cost W6 measured is the cost this shape removes.
"A verifier that has already confirmed one claim is no longer a fresh adversary for the next" No — and grouping makes it worse. Relevance-grouping puts the most correlated claims in one context, which is exactly where a confirmation carries over. This is the risk the WU accepts, and it is why the guardrail below is part of the requirement rather than advice.

A candidate proposed blocker never joins a batch. Blockers are the severity that decides a verdict and the class the corpus is thinnest on — three in 198 rounds. They keep one fresh agent each. Everything else the orchestrator may group.

The distinction the corpus supports is narrow and worth stating plainly: an epic stops owing a review of its own diff, and never stops owing that every seam in it carries one.

The bar, and why it moves

W6 pre-registered its bar as every known-real finding in the corpus is raised by the new fleet. No cut that removes scope can pass that bar — by construction, removing a dimension loses that dimension's findings. So the epic could adopt only cuts that lose literally nothing, and exactly one such cut exists: Gate 0's ordering. That is the mechanism by which three Working Units of measurement produced 60s.

This WU is judged on: no verified blocker or important is lost. Nits are out of the bar because DEC-47 already ruled they do not fail a verdict.

The priced loss, stated before the cut rather than discovered after it:
