The reading that kills it is R2, and it kills it on the epic's own terms. W8 left the round at 329s against a 300s target, with the remaining gap explicitly in the finder stage. W7 attacks the finder stage's membership. A stage that costs its slowest member does not get shorter when you remove other members — and on a corrective re-run the member you must keep is the one that raised the blocker. The cut is pointed at the right stage and the wrong axis of it.

R2 — the stage costs its slowest member

Hình 1 — the finder stage is where the remaining 29s has to come from, and it is the one stage W7's mechanism cannot shorten. Dropping four of five finders leaves the slowest one running.

The governing rule this uncovers. DEC-48 recorded that a cut which moves work between stages buys one axis with the other. W7 is the adjacent case and wants its own sentence: a cut which removes members from a parallel stage buys tokens, never wall-clock — the stage costs its slowest member, so the saving is real on the bill and zero on the clock unless the member removed is the slowest one. Every future "shrink the fleet" proposal owes this test before it owes an A/B.

R3 — the predicate that cannot be written

W7 needs a sound answer to "which dimensions can this delta reach?" There is none.

Candidate definitionWhy it fails
Re-run the dimensions whose prior findings touched the delta's files A dimension that found nothing in round 1 is unbounded — it has no files to compare against, and the new hunk is exactly where it might now find something
Re-run the dimensions that "own" the changed code No dimension owns a region. All five read the same bytes; the rubric splits them by question asked, not by file
Reuse a dimension's verdict when the delta is small Size is not safety. A one-line fix is the most likely place for a reuse or altitude defect, because it was written under pressure to satisfy one blocker
Reuse a verifier's verdict for an identical claim Soundest of the four, and still unsafe — a refutation rests on callers and surrounding code the delta may have moved. It also saves nothing on the clock, for R2's reason: the verifier stage is parallel too

And it contradicts the epic's own headline. DEC-45 binds a cr note to diff_sha256 over the whole code diff. A re-run that reuses four dimensions writes a note binding bytes that four of five dimensions never read. The note would claim more than the round did — which is the precise failure W4 was landed to end.

Stated rather than implied: the A/B did not run, and this result does not claim its answer. W7 is rejected on R1, R2 and R3, which are readings taken. R4 is not evidence that findings would escape — it is the reason nobody can say whether they would. Rejecting a cut because its test is unrunnable is honest; reporting an unrun test as a negative result would be the error W6 and W8 were both criticised for.
