Arm 1 — the verifier stage, and why it refused its own bar

Every labelled candidate in the repository was parsed out of 196 cr notes: 39 verified findings and 191 refuted claims. A pre-registered sample of 60 (seed 20260914 — every blocker and important in the corpus, six sampled nits, thirty refuted claims) was replayed through a fresh verifier, twice: once on Haiku 4.5 and once on Sonnet 5, same protocol, same commits, reading history through git show only.

Hình 2 — 120 agents, 60 paired cases. McNemar: 7 haiku-only-right against 6 sonnet-only-right. There is no difference to find.

The bar the plan pre-registered was not met — and was not met by the incumbent tier either. The plan required total agreement on candidates the incumbent verified as blocker or important, with one flip dropping L7 to the next model up. Haiku kept 21/24. Sonnet, the tier the rule escalates to, kept 22/24. Both refuse the same candidate.

A bar that the expensive model fails as surely as the cheap one is not measuring model risk; it is measuring replay noise. This is the exact failure the plan named in W6 — "no configuration of this review passes it, including the one in production, so it discriminates nothing" — reproduced here with a clean harness. So L7 is not adopted: a one-finding gap on n=24 cannot license the swap, and adopting against a failed pre-registered bar is the error W6 was criticised for. The measurement is carried forward; the follow-up owes a bar a fleet can pass.
