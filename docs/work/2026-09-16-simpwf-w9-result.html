The bar — no verified blocker or important is lost

Assessment against the corpus cases the retired dimensions owned. The three W6 simplification findings (D3, D4, O3) all read as nit under DEC-47's severity rows, and a nit never fails a verdict — so none of them is a blocker or important, and none is lost by the bar's terms. The reuse class (the tests/e2e_export.rs CA duplication) is the one place the bar is genuinely paid: duplication findings escalate to important under the rubric's uncertainty rule, and nothing else in the fleet catches them. That loss is real, it is priced in DEC-50, and the owner took it knowingly. Altitude's O1 crosses a module boundary, so the conditional keeps it.

Stated plainly rather than claimed away: the bar is met for simplification and for altitude, and is deliberately paid for reuse. A bar that hid that would be the failure W6 and W8 were both criticised for.

The projection is wrong, and the reason is the arithmetic behind it. DEC-50 priced a finder at W6's per-agent mean of 98.6k tokens and a verifier at W6's 52k. This round's finders cost 102k, 196k and 202k; its verifiers 126k, 150k and 157k. The two that ran long did so because they reproduced their findings — building the real binary, driving scratch repos, shimming git to observe what was spawned — rather than reading the diff and reasoning. W6's means were taken over rounds that mostly did not do that.

So the cut is real and the saving is smaller than claimed: removing two of five dimensions removed two agents and the verifiers they would have generated, but a round's cost is set at least as much by how hard its agents work as by how many there are. That is a reading DEC-49's rule did not anticipate — it governs the clock, and this is the token axis. One round is one reading; the rows above are what later rounds are compared against, not a conclusion.
