Round 1 raised three candidates and refuted all three. Nineteen candidates over seven rounds; nine verified, every one of them a false sentence in this WU's own report or notes rather than a line of code.

The pattern is the finding. Seven rounds found nothing wrong with the rename — the dimensions that judge code came back clean every time. What they found, nine times over, was this report claiming more than the tree supported — a total nobody counted, several counts that did not match their own lists, a check reported as passed in a form that could not be met, and a claim about what another document contained. Round 4's finding sat inside round 3's own correction, which is the sharpest version of the lesson: a sentence that summarises evidence is itself evidence, and re-stating it is a new claim, not a safer one. The two habits that ended it were dropping counts the document does not need and enumerating whatever count it keeps — and, after rounds 5 and 7, grepping every carrier of the claim for the retired wording, the git notes included, instead of correcting the places one happens to remember. A rename is easy to get right and easy to over-report, and the report is the part a human signs.
