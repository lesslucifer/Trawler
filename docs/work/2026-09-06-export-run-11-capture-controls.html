The three controls that change server state — take the capture out, bring one back, empty it — designed onto the flow list E-REDESIGN rebuilt. Run 10 answered these questions about a header of four text fields, a 24-entry registry and a collection with no dialog. All three have moved.

A′ is not a new zone — it is A's trailing cluster, and it now holds two things: one fixed-width muted slot and one 28px trigger. choose-before-act is the reason for that order and not the reverse: the filters decide what export and clear act on, the slot says what they decided ("6 flows"), and the trigger comes last. The slot also stays on screen while M is open — M opens 4px below it — so the scope is legible at the moment the act is chosen, which is where the Frame's each label states the scope it acts on is actually paid.

Why the three acts went behind one control, measured. The header is flex-wrap, so the question was never whether the controls fit at 1200px — it was the width below which A becomes two lines. Rendered and scanned in 2px steps:

A′A holds one line down tovs minWidth 800
none — the shipped header782pxfits, by 18px
three inline controls942pxtwo lines for the whole 800–942 band
one trigger, slot kept846pxtwo lines for 800–846
one trigger, no fixed slot814pxtwo lines for 800–814

So the inline cluster does not merely risk a second line, it guarantees one across a 142px band of allowed widths — and the shipped header had only 18px of slack at 800 to begin with, which is why every option above still needs something. The design takes the one that keeps the slot (846px) and asks W6 for minWidth: 850 in tauri.conf.json — one number, and the honest one: a six-column flow table at 800px leaves PATH 138px, so 800 was never a width this screen worked at. Dropping the slot instead would buy 32px and give back the reflow it exists to prevent.

What this costs, stated rather than buried. Export becomes two acts instead of one, and the Frame's goal-2 consequence changed with it — from the out-door is one act from the list to one named act, one level in. That is a real regression against a stated goal and it is the price of the row. What buys it back is the ranking those goals sit in: keep watching traffic is first, these three acts are the rarest things on the screen, and progressive-disclosure is explicit that the default view is the scan level with depth one invited act away. The argument that refused a second toolbar row — the least-used group must not take the most chrome — applies with more force to three permanent controls than it did to the row, because three controls cost 212.8px of the line the row was trying to protect.

The row is three groups, and the grouping is spacing rather than decoration. An earlier draft of this run spent one flat 12px gap between all eight members, so identity, query and acts read as a single queue — and then spent the row's only hairline on the smallest boundary in it (import against clear) while the largest (query against acts) went unmarked. Proximity carries the header: 8px inside A′, 20px at the two group seams. The additive/irreversible boundary is no longer a boundary in A at all — it moved into M, where it is a hairline that can actually be seen. That answers grouping-by-relatedness in both directions: no real boundary is left unmarked, and no separator marks a false one. It took two attempts — see T1.

The seam is not invented here. ui/src/App.tsx already sets mr-2 on the title, so the identity-to-query boundary is already a 20px seam against the row's 12px gap. The redesign reads that as the app's own convention and spends the same 20px at the second boundary, rather than minting a width of its own. Nothing the user already knows moves: A keeps its members and their order, and gains one slot and three controls after them.

One slot, two facts, no reflow. The slot holds the count at rest and the in-flight line while a transfer runs — "6 flows" becomes "exporting…" and comes back. Both are one muted line about the capture, both fit field-sm, and the slot's width is fixed, so neither its arrival nor its departure moves anything. Measured on the rendered assembly rather than asserted: in both states the slot is 80.0px and every member of A′ holds the same x — the slot at 866.2, ↓ at 954.2, ↑ at 990.2, clear at 1034.2 — which also confirms the gaps as 8px, 8px and 16px. The earlier draft inserted the in-flight line into the cluster instead, which re-allocated the header's slack and resized the filter field — a control the user may be typing in at that exact moment.

Why the second toolbar row is refused, measured rather than asserted. The devtools genre does put the transfers in a toolbar above the filter bar, so familiar-idioms owes a driver for departing from it. The driver is not cost: a chrome row of 28px controls at py-chrome-y is 37px against a 30px row, so it costs about one flow — not the four an earlier draft of the Frame claimed, which was asserted rather than measured and is wrong by roughly threefold. The driver is visual-weight-follows-goals: keep watching traffic is the top goal and these three acts are the rarest thing on the screen, so a permanent zone of their own would hand the least-used group the most new chrome. DevTools earns its second row with a dozen controls; three do not earn one.

What the redesign changed here. Host and method are Selects, so two of A's members now open popups. That is why A′ is at the trailing edge rather than merely somewhere in A: a popup opens below its trigger and the cluster must not be the thing it covers. Run 10 reached the same placement against four plain boxes — the answer holds, but it now holds for a second reason the earlier run could not have given.

IconButton
T1		 A′ · the ⋮ trigger
reuse
One icon-only trigger; the acts it opens are words.
why

Lands in @use "icon-only triggers unambiguous in place". Clears @avoid "primary actions (no visual weight at rest)": the trigger is not an action at all, it opens the ones that are. And it clears the line that decided the earlier draft — "actions with no fitting glyph (word label carries it better)" — because the three acts do carry their words now, inside Menu. One control needs a face; three did not have one between them.

Why a bare square glyph and not a worded trigger. A worded trigger with a caret — capture ▾ — was the first instinct and it is wrong here for a specific reason: it would stand immediately beside two Selects that look exactly like that, so it would read as a third filter. The row's two affordance families are "bordered box with a caret = narrows the list" and "bare square = acts", and the trigger has to be legible as the second. That is grouping-by-relatedness applied to affordance rather than to spacing.

Why ⋮ and not ⋯. The app already spends the horizontal ellipsis on in progress — "exporting…", and StatusPill renders "…" for a flow with no response yet. A horizontal ellipsis as a control face would collide with a meaning the screen already carries, in the same header, sometimes in the same second. The vertical one has no such tenant. One proposed entry, slug more.

Run 10 and this run's earlier drafts put three faces in the header — ⇧ ⇩ ⌫, then ↓ ↑ and a word. The first set was worse than it looked: all three are keyboard-key symbols on the platform this app ships to (⇧ is Shift, ⌫ is Backspace), so the cluster read as a shortcut hint, and ⇧ pointed the opposite way from the app's own save face for the same act. The second set was correct and still cost 212.8px of a row that had 18px of slack at minWidth. Collapsing to one trigger retires the whole question: the only face left is the one that opens the menu.

TonedText
T1		 A′ · the slot
reuse
One fixed slot holding two facts: the count, then the in-flight line.
why

IconButton composes Button, whose @states is explicit: no loading state of its own — for a slow action the caller MUST render both halves, the control disabled for the whole in-flight window AND a progress line naming what is awaited. Export and import are both slow, so the line is a contract, not a decoration.

Lands in @use "any short value whose meaning is carried by tone". Clears @avoid "a toned sentence or prose with an action" — "exporting…" is a value, not a sentence.

It is a slot, not an insertion. The same field-sm box carries "6 flows" at rest and "exporting…" while a transfer runs, right-aligned against the doors it scopes. Fixing the width is the whole point: the earlier draft inserted the line into the cluster as a new member, so starting an export re-allocated the header's slack and resized the filter Field — a control the user may be mid-keystroke in. Two facts share the box because they are the same kind of fact — one muted line about the capture — and because the count is what the transfer is transferring, so replacing it loses nothing the user needs during the flight. It takes text-control rather than the inherited 14px so it reads at the size of the controls it sits among (Tune T6).

Tone is muted: in-flight is neither success nor failure, and the chromatic set has no third meaning for it. Notice is the right shape for the same job inside a dialog (where there is room for a sentence, and where ProxyCloseDialog already does exactly this) and the wrong shape in a control row, where its glyph and block would compete with the filters.

Button
T1		 E, F · the answers
reuse
A word each: the escape bare, the act at the default frame.
why

Lands in @use "any action that needs a word and is not a copy". The interesting clearance is @avoid "emphasis by styling: pick which actions to offer instead, a Button never carries a primary/filled variant" — so the destructive answer cannot be made red or heavy, which is the convention most confirm dialogs reach for. The component has no such variant to reach for: its frames are border and bare and its tones body and mute, so the only styling choice left is which answer to de-emphasise. The alarm lives in the question, not on a button.

Also clears @avoid "warn-toning it inside a warn Notice (the message owns the alarm, the button is the way out)" — that line describes this exact composition, so the design follows it rather than fighting it. ProxyCloseDialog is the shipped precedent, and the shape is asymmetric: the escape is frame="bare" tone="mute" and the act takes the defaults, with the alarm in the prose. Both asks here follow it — cancel and "choose a file…" bare, clear and import at the default — so the screen carries one button idiom rather than inventing a second.

Notice
T1		 E, F · question, refusal, in-flight
reuse
One sentence at a tone, action slot left empty.
why

The ask splits its words: the panel's title asks the question and carries the scope ("clear 214 flows?"), and the Notice carries the consequence ("the capture is not saved anywhere") at tone="warn" — a failure-or-alarm sentence, the tone's fixed meaning. The split is forced rather than chosen: the frame always renders a title, so putting the whole question in the Notice would say it twice. The refusal ("unsupported flag --data-urlencode") is warn too. Both clear @avoid "transient confirmations of an action" — neither is transient: the ask stands until answered, the refusal until the paste changes.

The in-flight line inside the dialog lands in @use "any zone that has nothing to show and must say which nothing it is (empty · pending · capped · not captured)" at tone="mute" — the shape ProxyCloseDialog ships for its own slow remedy.

C's absences are three, not two. ui/src/FlowList.tsx ships two — "no flows match this filter" and "no flows yet — send traffic through the proxy" — with a comment saying they must not share a sentence, because telling someone to send traffic they have already sent is the failure the second exists to prevent. A cleared capture is exactly that person. So clearing lands on a third: "capture cleared", detail "send traffic through the proxy — 127.0.0.1:7420" — the same imperative voice both siblings use ("clear a field to widen the search" · "send traffic through the proxy — …"), and the same remedy as the never-captured one, because after a clear the remedy genuinely is the same. The heading is what separates them, and it is the half that says which absence this is. W6 adds the third case to Absence; reusing the never-captured wording would re-introduce the bug that component's comment guards against. Run 10 answered this the other way — its outcome table and its empty-states-orient line both had a clear land on the existing "no flows yet — send traffic through the proxy". That answer is overturned here, and the thing that overturned it is a build fact run 10 did not cite: the comment in FlowList.tsx saying the two shipped absences must not share a sentence, for precisely the reason a cleared list makes live.

The action slot stays empty on purpose. Notice can hold a Button, which would make the whole ask one component. Rejected: the ask needs two answers, the slot takes one, and a cancel that is not a peer of the act is a dark pattern.

Menu
T2 → proposed		 M · the three acts
propose
Nothing in 28 entries is an action menu, and Select is not one.
why

Select is the near miss and it fails on its own @avoid twice, independently. "Two to four options (use SegmentedControl — this is a miss by cardinality, not kind)": three acts is below its floor. And "multi-select or a clear affordance — at most one option is selected, and nothing returns to unselected once chosen": a menu of acts has no selected value at all, so the whole notion of a held selection is wrong for it, not merely inconvenient. SegmentedControl is worse — it shows all choices at once, which is the chrome this design is trying not to spend.

What it must own, and all of it exists already in Select's popup: the positioned surface (canvas, hairline, rounded-card, shadow-pop, z-popup, offset by chrome-y), ↑↓ to move without acting, Enter to act, Esc to close unchanged, Tab to close and move on, and focus returning to the trigger on every exit. Those are the rulebook's own keyboard conventions, so this run invents no interaction — which is why keyboard-parity and focus-returns-to-origin are answered by the proposal rather than by this screen.

What it must not own: a selected state, a tick column, type-to-filter, or a scrim. It is not modal — clicking away dismisses it, and the list underneath stays readable, which is the whole difference between M and E.

The overlap is the interesting part of the proposal. Every behaviour above is already implemented inside Select, so the honest recommendation to the owner is to look at both at once: either Menu duplicates a proven popup shell, or that shell is extracted and both consume it. This run is not entitled to decide which — it has one sighting of a menu — so it names the choice instead of silently taking the cheaper half.

Dialog
T2 → proposed		 E, F · the modal frame
propose
The frame exists and ships; it is not in the collection yet.
why

ModalShell in ui/src/ProxyCloseDialog.tsx owns the scrim, the focus trap, Esc (guarded on defaultPrevented, so a Select closing its popup is not the dialog closing), backdrop dismiss, focus return to the invoker, and initial focus on whichever control the caller marks — falling back to the panel's first focusable element, which with a title row is the ✕. That is precisely what this run needs, twice.

It is screen-local by an explicit warrant in its own source: "no registry entry owns modality, and a general Dialog drawn from the app's only dialog would generalise from a sample of one." That warrant is a promise about a condition, and this run is the condition changing — the clear ask and the paste are sightings two and three, on a different screen, wanting the same behaviour unchanged. Promoting it is the vocabulary's own rule (reuse-or-propose, second-sighting), so it is proposed here and performed by its owner.

Run 10 proposed AskDialog — a component to be invented, described by what it must own. The proposal is now a promotion of working code with a focus trap that has survived a WebDriver suite. Same verdict word, materially different risk, and the difference is worth stating rather than letting the two runs read as if nothing happened.

What this run needs from it and does not re-decide: the scrim, the trap, Esc returning focus to the trigger, the initial-focus rule. What it must not own: the question's wording or tone — those arrive as a Notice.

the import mark
T0 inline		 C · cell 1, leading
build local
A glyph inside the time cell's width — no column, no component.
why

Three candidates weighed against data-ink-first, whose test is: strip it in imagination, is information lost? A seventh column fails — 110px of every row spent on a value that is captured for ~100% of rows in a normal session, and the row cannot grow forbids the width anyway. A tinted row fails colour-plus-text and would collide with the selected wash. A glyph in the leading cell costs nothing when absent and is the convention a devtools user already reads.

Not a component: one conditional character with an accessible label, in one cell of one screen. Proposing an entry on a sample of one is the more expensive mistake — and this run makes the opposite call for Dialog precisely because there the sample is three.

The mark wears the door's own face. It is ↑, the same glyph as the import control, because that is the control that put the row there — one face, one meaning, learnable in a single sighting. The earlier draft used ⇩, which was not only a keyboard-key symbol but pointed the opposite way from the door it came from.

The text channel satisfying colour-plus-text is the glyph's title/aria-label ("imported") — a text channel on its own, and the only one this design ships. The sentence that stood here read "plus the — a text channel on its own": a phrase had been lost in an earlier edit and the broken clause survived several rounds of review. It deliberately does not rest on a ?source= filter: the capability exists on the API and this run adds no control for it, so citing one would be citing something the screen does not have. The mark is ink-mute, deliberately not chromatic, because being imported is not an outcome.

Why the list refills from the bus, not from the response. useFlows refetches on any /stream event it is subscribed to, and import and clear both announce themselves there. Refilling from the mutation's own response would give the screen two sources of truth for the same list and a race between them — and the responses carry counts, not rows, so they could not refill it anyway. One build fact this depends on: ui/src/api.ts does not yet subscribe to cleared, which the server does emit, so W6 must add it or the clear will empty the store and leave the list on screen.

Initial focus follows the act only where the act is corrective. ModalShell focuses whatever the caller marks, and ProxyCloseDialog marks "turn it off" — a button that repairs a machine the user would otherwise leave stranded, which is why its comment reasons about not putting the dismissal under the first Enter. That reasoning does not transfer to a door that destroys: here the marker goes on [cancel], so the reflexive Enter is the one that does nothing. The Frame's clearing is irreversible constraint decides it, and the rulebook does not — its keyboard conventions say Enter activates what is focused, never which control a destructive ask should focus. This is a per-caller placement of the existing marker, not a rule ModalShell must special-case. focus-returns-to-origin stays the component's guarantee, not this screen's.
