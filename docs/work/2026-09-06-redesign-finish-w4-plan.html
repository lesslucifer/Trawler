The shell and the flow list, second pass. Four deferrals came out of E-REDESIGN addressed to a later design WU: the pane's width, the status filter's control, the statement the list cannot make when the API is unreachable, and whether DEC-34's payload cell has had its second sighting. Requirement

One design run over the app shell and the flow list resolves all four deferred questions on the record — the detail pane's width, the control the status filter uses to express a class, where and in what words an unreachable API is stated, and whether the truncating payload cell has earned a registry entry — each with the alternatives it rejected and the evidence that decided it, Tier-1 green and Tier-3 judged, so that W5 widens the API to a contract a judged design asked for and W6 builds against a design rather than against four deferrals, and so that a reader who was not here can check every ruling against the run instead of against the diff.

The two ways this WU goes wrong

Risk

A receipt that is complete and false

Seventeen slugs are easy to satisfy in words. The guard is that every satisfied must point at something in the artifact a reviewer can look at — a zone, a cell, a sentence — and that the three verdicts which are not satisfaction (n/a, violated, blocked) are used where they are true. The W3 run's two n/as and one violated are the precedent.

Risk

Designing what W6 must build, badly

Every ruling here becomes code in W6 with no second design pass. So each of the four answers must be buildable from the run alone — a width that names its token, a control that names its component and its props, a statement that names its zone, its tone and its words. A ruling W6 has to re-decide is a ruling this run did not finish.
