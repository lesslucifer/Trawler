E-SIMPDF — the design flow, re-costed

Six staged artifacts, a close step and a forced-coverage receipt over every soft rule — priced once at design time, never against what fifteen closed runs actually produced. Measure the yield, cut what does not pay, publish the bill.

Draft v1 · 2026-09-16 · Trawler · T3 epic plan · E-SIMPDF

Intent

The design flow was built by E-DFLOW and has never been re-costed. E-SIMPWF did exactly this for the code review and left three rules behind it — DEC-48 (a round is bought on the clock and the bill), DEC-49 (a cut is priced against a corpus before it is taken) and DEC-50 (carry only the dimensions that pay) — but it simplified nothing about the design flow itself, and said so. Today every screen design pays for six staged artifacts, a five-list close, a receipt that must carry a verdict for all 20 soft slugs in the rulebook whether or not the screen ever met them, and three tiers of judgment. Nobody has asked what each of those returns. Now, because the roadmap's next UI work (E-ONBOARD, E-REPLAY's composer) runs this flow again at full price, and because the corpus needed to answer the question already exists: 15 closed runs and 25 component design files sitting in git history. The epic's product is a measurement first and a cut second — and a measurement that refuses a cut is a result, not a failure.

Use-case requirements

A process epic — it delivers no UC-* behaviour and changes none. No proxy behaviour, no API surface and no shipped screen moves; this epic designs nothing and builds nothing a user can see. What changes is the contract an AI agent follows when it does design a screen, so the truth lands in T2 decisions (the receipt cut and the stage cut, each its own record, next free number after DEC-52), in the T2 rulebook where a slug's coverage obligation is stated (design-rules-index, governed by DEC-14), and in the T1 design flow spec, which is rule-bearing text and is therefore edited under a Reviewed-By trailer.

Also out of scope, for the same reason — they change the flow rather than price it:

  • No new design rules and no rulebook growth. A slug may lose its forced-coverage obligation; no slug is added, and the closed vocabulary (DEC-17) is untouched.
  • No screen is designed. W5 re-runs the flow over a screen the corpus already holds, as a paired measurement; its artifact is evidence, never a shipped design, and it hands nothing to a build.
  • The code review's own shape is not reopened. E-SIMPWF owns it, and its open measurements are its own backlog rows.
  • No hard rule and no Tier-1 predicate is relaxed for cost. Hard rules are the cheap half by construction; if the measurement says otherwise, that is a finding for a later epic, not a cut here.

The rule this epic owes itself. DEC-49 says a cut is priced against a corpus before it is taken, and E-SIMPWF.W9's own round proved the sharper half: its projection was 2.3× optimistic because a station's cost is set as much by how hard its agent works as by how many stations there are. So W1 counts yield from the archive, which history can answer, and W5 measures cost live, which only a re-run can. A projected saving is never reported as a saving.

E-SIMPDF.W1 — the corpus, counted

Walk every closed design run in git history — 15 named runs plus the two candidates 2026-09-04-flow-list-shell.html and 2026-09-06-shell-list-second-pass.html, whose status this WU settles — and the 25 component design files, and record what each station of the flow produced. One row per stage, per loop-back, per close step and per soft slug; per row, across the corpus: how often it changed the design, caught a violation, raised a proposal, raised a blocked-on entry, or returned nothing. Verdict distribution per slug (satisfied · inherited · n/a · blocked · violated …) is the receipt's half; downstream citation — did any later stage or Tier-3 finder ever refer to this artifact — is the stages' half. No verdicts and no cuts: this WU publishes numbers and the commands that reproduce them.

E-SIMPDF.W2 — the receipt, re-costed

Decide, slug by slug against W1's distribution, which of the rulebook's 20 soft rules keep their forced-coverage obligation and which move to a cheaper form or leave the receipt. The live question is what inherited is worth: the flow calls it "a first-class verdict, not a dodge", and a slug that reads inherited in every run of the corpus is a row that costs an author a line and a reviewer a read while asserting nothing about the screen. The counter-case is the reason forced coverage exists at all — "a receipt that only lists the rules the design happened to think about proves nothing" — so a cut must show the slug is structurally inapplicable, never merely quiet. Lands as a decision record stating the surviving set, the rejected alternatives, and the exact predicate W4 implements.

E-SIMPDF · the receipt half of expected result 2 · amends the coverage obligation the rulebook (DEC-14) states, without touching any rule's content.

A numbered T2 decision naming which slugs stay forced, which do not, and the evidence row behind each call — or, if the distribution supports no cut, that finding stated with its numbers (expected result 5, receipt half). The rulebook's own coverage statement is edited to match. No checker change lands here.

E-SIMPDF.W1. Independent of W3 — either may land first.

E-SIMPDF.W3 — the stages and the close, re-costed

The same judgment over the six staged artifacts, the two loop-backs and the five-step close. The questions W1's table answers: which stage's output was ever read by a downstream stage or cited by a Tier-3 finder; whether either loop-back ever fired; whether the predecessor walk (DEC-40) ever caught a dropped answer, which is the single thing it exists to catch; and whether any two stages are answering the same question at two prices — Elements' fit trace against Tune's before/after being the obvious suspects. A stage may be merged rather than cut; the cheapest available result is two artifacts becoming one. Lands as a decision record with the surviving sequence.

E-SIMPDF · the stage half of expected result 2 · rewrites the sequence the T1 design flow spec states.

A numbered T2 decision naming the surviving stages, merges and close steps with the evidence row behind each, and what a run written to it looks like — or that the corpus supports no cut, with its numbers. No spec or checker change lands here.

E-SIMPDF.W1. Independent of W2 — either may land first.

E-SIMPDF.W4 — the spec, the rulebook and the checker move together

Land W2's and W3's decisions in the three places that must never disagree: the T1 design flow spec (rule-bearing — a Reviewed-By trailer), the rulebook's coverage obligation, and verify-doc's predicates in xtask/src/doc.rs together with their failure output, which the flow names as the agent's oracle. A cut station's predicates are removed, not left to pass vacuously; a merged stage gets the union of its parents' checks. The flow e2e is rewritten to the surviving sequence — same property, new stage list: after every stage the run is red for exactly the stages not yet written.

If W2 and W3 both concluded "no cut", this WU collapses to the documentation of that finding.

E-SIMPDF.W2 and E-SIMPDF.W3 — both decisions, since spec and checker land in one commit.

E-SIMPDF.W5 — the paired re-run, and the bill

The arm only a live run can supply. Re-design one screen the corpus already holds, under the new shape, end to end — the same screen, so the two arms are paired and the comparison is against a known artifact rather than a projection. Measure both axes DEC-48 names, clock and tokens, and publish them against what the archived original shows. The output is evidence: the re-run's artifact is not handed to any build and retires with the WU. Then the epic closes — distillation, and the seven inherited items re-homed as their own backlog row.

E-SIMPDF · expected result 4 · the measured half of DEC-48/49 that W1's archive walk structurally cannot provide.

Expected result 4 holds. A stated saving (or cost) in seconds and tokens from one paired run, with the sample size — one — stated as plainly as the number, per the 2.3× correction E-SIMPWF.W9 recorded against its own projection. A re-run that comes out more expensive is reported as found.

E-SIMPDF.W4 — there is no new shape to run until it lands.

E2E test plan

The epic's end-to-end proof is the flow harness that already exists — xtask/tests/e2e_design_flow.rs, feature-gated behind design-e2e — rewritten in W4 to the surviving sequence. It is the right e2e because the flow's whole point is the sequence: a fake designing agent emits one artifact at a time, a fake human answers each, and all three tiers judge the result over a real git tree.

Asserts, under the new shapeThe failure it refuses
After every surviving stage, the run is red for exactly the surviving stages not yet writtena cut station still being demanded, or a kept one silently dropped — the vacuous-green failure mode
The receipt is complete against the surviving slug set and red against the old oneW2's cut landing in the spec but not in the predicate, or the reverse
The rulebook moving under an open run still re-opens the close's re-pina receipt complete against a rulebook nobody has read since — the property must survive the cut
A rule-violating mockup still reds at the stage that wrote ita breach surfacing only at the end, where it is expensive

Per the testing standard, the lower levels are additive and non-negotiable: W4's predicate changes carry unit tests in xtask/src/doc.rs; W2, W3 and W5 are measurement and decision WUs whose diffs change no code, so their test evidence is waived with that reason recorded on the note. W1 is likewise docs-only unless its counting is scripted, in which case the script carries its own unit tests.
