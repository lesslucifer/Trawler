No nesting — and what variant="band" does instead W5 · F3
speca Section must never render inside another Section's content slot. Two grouping levels are reached the other way: a variant="band" Section takes its members in a dedicated group slot that adds no padding and no heading of its own, so the members' own padding and hairlines are the only ones. Depth is capped at two — a band inside a band, or a band inside a content slot, is the same violation as before. whythe seed card's avoid line ("nesting a Section inside a Section") names a symptom — doubled padding and collapsed heading levels — not a topology. The band form avoids both: padding does not double because the group slot contributes none, and the levels do not collapse because the band is h2 and its members are h3. What the original rule was protecting is intact; what it was accidentally forbidding (any second level at all) is what blocked two screens (r2·3, r3·16)

Section owns no interactive states. No hover, focus, active, disabled, loading, or empty state of its own — the seed card explicitly records "states: none of its own". Any state visible inside a Section belongs to its child. This is what it means to be layout chrome.
No collapsible state. The HeaderTable's "collapsible headers" disclosure (from the W2 roster seed) is owned by HeaderTable, not by Section. Section never collapses — if a group of content needs to collapse, a disclosure element wraps that content inside the slot. Section is unaware of it.

Default: <section> root + <h2>. Each Section is a landmark region; a screen-reader user navigates panel zones by heading. The shipped code uses <section> + <h2> (FlowDetail.tsx:7–12).

Second form: variant="band". A band renders its label as the group's <h2> and the Sections it contains as <h3>. Two grouping levels, and still no nesting — the No-nesting rule bans a Section inside another Section's content slot, which is where the padding doubles and the heading levels collapse. A band's members sit in a dedicated group slot that carries no padding of its own: the band contributes the label and the outer hairline, the members contribute their own. Exactly two levels exist. A band inside a band is still forbidden, and that is what keeps the heading hierarchy finite.

alt: free level prop (h2/h3/h4) rejected — still

An open-ended heading level is what the original F3 rejected, and it stays rejected: it makes every call site decide a level, which is how heading hierarchies drift, and it implies arbitrary nesting depth. variant="band" gives the two levels a real screen needs and refuses the third — the level is derived from the variant, never passed.

alt: the screen renders the band itself rejected

What run #3 actually did — its zone label became screen-owned markup above the Section stack (r3·16), and run #2's two-leg panel would have needed the same. Rejected because it makes group-level heading typography a per-screen decision, which is the exact drift this component exists to prevent (frame driver ①). A label that every stacking screen must draw itself is a component gap, not a screen responsibility.

Why F3 was reopened W5 · D5 — edited in place, no new fork record

Two runs hit the same wall from opposite directions, and neither could cite this file for an answer:
runwhat it neededwhat it did instead
r2·3two grouping levels for a two-leg panel (leg → zone)filed the fork as blocking: either flat zoning with the leg living in repeated heading words, or a second heading-typography file forked off Section
r3·16a label for one Section holding three preconditionsput "set up capture" in screen-owned markup above the stack — because .lbl labels a block, and a stack of blocks has no label slot

Both are one gap: Section owned exactly one level, so the second level had nowhere to go. r3·16 states the general form — the zone label of a stacked Section is otherwise always the screen's problem, in every screen that ever stacks one. variant="band" is the answer to both: it is the leg level in r2·3's panel and the stack label in r3·16's zone B, and it makes the answer citable instead of re-derived. Per W5 D5 the fork card above is edited in place; the snapshot history and the doc diff layer are the record of what moved.

Use / avoid

✅ Use

Every labelled zone of a detail or settings screen: "Request", "Response headers", "Body", any titled region where the heading + hairline + padding rhythm must hold consistently across sibling zones. Current tenants: the five <Section> calls in FlowDetail.tsx:80–116. variant="band" when a screen needs a second grouping level — a two-leg panel's REQUEST / RESPONSE level, or a label over a stack of Sections (F3).

🚫 Avoid

Unlabelled grouping — plain py-3 spacing does it. A Section inside another Section's content slot, and a band inside a band — the heading hierarchy collapses and padding doubles; two levels is the cap, and the second one is variant="band", never nesting. Drawing a group label yourself above a Section stack — that is the band's job, and a screen that draws it forks the heading typography this component owns (r3·16). Any group that needs to collapse or toggle visibility — put the disclosure element inside the content slot instead; Section itself never collapses. A page-level heading — <h1> belongs to the panel's identity bar, not here.
