1 · Frame

Purpose: someone who just launched Trawler makes their machine trust it and route traffic through it, until a real request proves it works. Given (functional input — received, not designed): install the CA · probe CA trust · set the system proxy · probe the proxy · unset the proxy · observe decrypted traffic · observe any traffic · guided steps per target · copyable prove-it command Not given (may not be assumed to exist): per-step completion the app can observe · a reason a handshake failed · remote-device attribution · undo of a CA install · progress inside an install

2 · Layout

Two legs across the width, because the screen has two jobs at once: track three steps, and work the one that is unfinished. The shipped column stacked them and had room for neither.

scroll / overflow: A and D are pinned and never scroll · B is capped at three
                   steps by STEPS and cannot grow, so it never scrolls · C is
                   the only scrolling region — its guided steps are unbounded
                   (StepList renders what it is handed and owns no cap of its
                   own, so this screen caps C, not the component)

Zone C is the whole reason for the split: it holds a step's action, its verdict and its manual fallback at once, which is what the stacked column could not do without pushing the other steps off screen. B stays visible so a stalled user can always see which of the three is unfinished.

▸ why
  • Rail verdict under the name — a leading glyph made the tone the row's first thing while the step's identity came second, and a colour-carried glyph alone is a second channel only by accident. Putting the name first and the resolver's own sentence under it gives every verdict its text channel and ranks the rail by what the user is looking for. Loops back to Layout: no.
  • Evidence label demoted — "run this command to generate your first request" restates what zone D plainly is once the command is visible. One uppercase-mute label and the payload. Loops back to Layout: no.
  • Manual steps open on failure — the first draft left the disclosure closed in every state, so a failed click ended in a dead end with the way out one hunt away. Opening it exactly when the app's own action failed (or cannot run) is the Frame's "the way out sits inside the failing step". Loops back to Layout: no — C3 already existed as a zone; only its default state changed.
  • Title band, not a hero — a centred serif hero was the shipped screen's answer to a mostly empty window, and it survives into a wide layout as pure non-data pixels. A band label sets the job in one line and hands the width to the two legs. Loops back to Layout: no.
